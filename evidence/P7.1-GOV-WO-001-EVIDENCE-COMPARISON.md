## Decision Quality Assessment

### Question: Did governed execution materially improve Owner visibility, provenance, custody, or decision quality?

**Answer: YES — with significant qualification.**

The governance INTENT produced measurable improvements even when the governance TOOLS were unavailable:

1. **Owner visibility:** PRE-work authorization, MID-work heartbeat, POST-work structured comparison. Direct editing had none of these.

2. **Provenance:** Machine-verifiable chain from Flightplan session → work order → evidence doc → test case → result packet → validation results → this comparison. Direct editing had only a manual note.

3. **Custody:** Flightplan provides partial custody chain. Librarian custody chain was unavailable. Direct editing had neither.

4. **Decision quality:** Owner has schema validation results (5/5 + 7/7), tool boundary findings (3 errors documented), and a structured comparison to inform the next decision. Direct editing would provide only "files were updated."

### Significant Qualification

The Librarian session tools were unavailable. This means:
- No governed checkout/checkin lifecycle
- No Librarian heartbeat record
- No Librarian receipt trail
- Custody chain is partial (Flightplan only)

The improvements observed are **governance-intent improvements**, not **governance-tool improvements**. The agent's discipline of recording evidence before proceeding — even when tools failed — is what produced the improved Owner visibility.

This is still P7.1 evidence: governance discipline (stopping at boundaries, recording evidence) produces better Owner decisions than direct editing, even when governance tools are broken.
